# Lean Digital Marketing Strategy - Reports that Matter

## Executive summary

Reports that Matter is a discovery and access layer for serious public‑interest reports that are currently hard to find, poorly linked, and effectively unusable on the modern web. Many such reports are locked inside large PDFs, scattered across broken or obscure websites, and referenced indirectly rather than accessed directly.

The core strategy is to unlock the latent value of these reports by extracting compelling excerpts, creating deep links into specific sections of the original documents, and distributing those excerpts through existing platforms where attention already exists. Traffic is expected to land directly inside reports (at highlighted passages), where users are given context, light engagement options, and a soft path to subscription.

The strategy is deliberately lean: one to two days of setup using AI‑assisted pipelines, followed by approximately one hour per week of ongoing activity. The focus is awareness and discovery first; retention, partnerships, and monetisation follow only after traction is visible.

## 1. Strategic framing

### Purpose

Make high‑quality public‑interest reports discoverable, linkable, and usable in the internet age.

### Core problem

* Important reports exist but are:

  * difficult to discover,
  * poorly or inconsistently linked,
  * fragmented across multiple sites,
  * locked inside long, unsearchable PDFs,
  * unusable on mobile and for citation.
* Even when journalists or institutions reference them, users are often not taken to the report itself, let alone the relevant passage.

### What Reports that Matter is

* A discovery and access layer for serious reports.
* A way to surface, excerpt, and link directly to the most informative parts of those reports.

## 2. Positioning and brand posture

### Positioning (plain language)

A way to find, link to, and actually read the most important parts of serious public‑interest reports.

### Tone

* Curious and exploratory.
* Evidence‑first.
* Minimal opinion, maximal signal.

### Brand implications

* Avoid heavy visual branding.
* Prioritise clarity, credibility, and consistency.
* Let the source material speak for itself.

## 3. SMART goals (first 6 months)

### Awareness

* Increase monthly unique visitors by 50–100% from the current baseline.
* Achieve regular external uptake (at least 5–10 third‑party shares, mentions, or references per month).

### Discoverability

* Publish 20+ reports with:

  * exposed full text,
  * paragraph‑level permalinks,
  * short, indexable summaries.

### Engagement

* At least 10% of visitors interact with highlighted sections (reshare or light feedback such as “interesting”).

### Conversion

* Reach 300–500 email subscribers opting in for updates when new reports are surfaced.

## 4. Top‑of‑funnel awareness mechanics

### Acquisition principle

* Meet people where attention already exists.
* Do not attempt to build a new audience from scratch.

### Distribution channels

Channels are selected based on whether they:

* reward primary sources,
* allow outbound links,
* host journalists, researchers, and civic actors,
* surface evidence and facts rather than hot takes.

Initial channels to test (maximum two at any one time):

* Twitter / X
* Reddit (carefully chosen policy, legal, economics, or investigative subreddits)

Secondary tests, if signal warrants:

* LinkedIn
* Substack Notes (as a distribution layer, not a newsletter)

## 5. Core growth engine: extraction and linking pipeline

### Raw asset

* Existing public‑interest reports (PDFs or hosted online).

### Automation‑first approach

Automatic ingestion is preferred over manual annotation.

Pipeline:

1. Ingest report files or source URLs.
2. Use AI to extract:

   * salient paragraphs,
   * key findings,
   * striking statistics or factual claims.
3. Generate:

   * short, neutral annotations,
   * a concise “why this report matters” summary.
4. Create:

   * paragraph‑level permalinks into the report,
   * highlighted sections as first‑class entry points,
   * optional image previews (quote cards, tables, charts).

The excerpt does not replace the report; it points directly into it.

## 6. Content and distribution units

### Smallest repeatable unit

* Annotated excerpt consisting of:

  * a quoted passage or statistic,
  * 1–3 sentences of neutral context,
  * a deep link to the highlighted section of the report.

### Optional extensions

* One‑paragraph link posts explaining why a report matters.
* Short auto‑generated summaries for discovery contexts.

All content should be batch‑generated and scheduled in advance.

## 7. Landing and conversion design

### Where users land

* Inside the report itself, at a highlighted and linkable section.

### Primary actions

* View report context (short introduction or summary).
* Re‑share the highlighted excerpt.

### Secondary actions

* Lightweight feedback (e.g. “interesting”).
* Subscribe to updates.

### Funnel logic

* Curiosity → orientation → light signal → subscription.
* No hard calls to action on first contact.

## 8. Loyalty and retention

### Email list role

* Notification channel, not a newsletter.
* Low frequency.
* Clear value proposition: alerts when new important reports are surfaced.

Retention is intentionally lightweight at this stage.

## 9. Execution plan under constraints

### Setup (1–2 days)

* Finalise positioning sentence.
* Build AI extraction and annotation workflow.
* Implement:

  * report summary blocks,
  * paragraph‑level permalinks,
  * sharing and light engagement signals.

### Ongoing (≈1 hour per week)

* Select and schedule 3–5 excerpts.
* Post to 1–2 distribution channels.
* Observe which excerpts travel and refine extraction heuristics accordingly.

---

## Appendix A: Monetisation (deferred)

Monetisation is explicitly downstream of trust and attention.

Potential options once traction exists:

* Soft donations.
* “Sponsor a report” or “fund the surfacing of a report” model.

No paywalls, subscriptions, or aggressive asks at this stage.

## Appendix B: If more capacity becomes available

### Enhancements

* Visual excerpts as a standard output.
* Clustering and linking related reports.
* Public signals such as “most shared” or “most cited”.
* Lightweight editorial curation (e.g. weekly surfaced insight).

### Strategic extension

* Explore partnerships with journalism, research, or civic organisations only after sustained external uptake is visible.
