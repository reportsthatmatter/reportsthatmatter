# Brand voice: worked examples

Concrete before-and-after examples from Rufus's feedback, with why. [`brand-voice.md`](brand-voice.md) holds the rules; this file shows them applied. Add to it whenever Rufus corrects a piece of copy: the before, his version, and the reason in a sentence or two. Newest first.

## The tone we want

Factual and compelling at once. Not cautious and hedged, and not punchy and "AI-y". The model is a good journalist's opening: a true, concrete story that makes you want to know why, then a plain statement of what the document is.

## 2026-11-22: archive taglines (the lines under each report on / and /reports)

**Round 1, too cautious** (a critique pass had removed anything not stated word for word in a source):

> The committee that funded NASA investigates the Challenger accident, and names Congress among those who pushed NASA to fly too often.

**Round 2, too clever, "AI-y"** (written to sound punchy):

> Congress investigates the shuttle it paid for, and finds itself among those who pushed NASA to fly too often.

Why it fails: it assumes the reader already knows what the Challenger accident was, and it leads with a twist ("finds itself") instead of the event. It sounds written, not reported.

**Rufus's version:**

> On 28 January 1986 the Space Shuttle Challenger broke apart 73 seconds after lifting off from Kennedy Space Center, killing its crew of seven. This is Congress's own investigation of the disaster, written by the committee that authorized and funded NASA.

Why it works:

- **It starts with a story, and an immediately compelling one.** A date, a place, what happened, the human cost. "It sounds like the start of a film": you want to know why.
- **Then it says plainly what this document is.** "This is Congress's own investigation…": who wrote it, and why that is interesting (the committee that funded NASA).
- **The facts come from our own introduction** (`editorial/<id>.yaml` `background`), so they are already checked. No adjectives; the events carry it.

The pattern for every report: *[dated event, concrete, with its human or historical stakes]. This is [who]'s [what], [the one thing that makes this document special].*

Other lessons from the same round:

- **Journalistic, not cautious.** "Minute by minute" for the 9/11 Commission's account is fine: a journalist would say it, and it is true in substance. Accuracy means being right, not quoting the source's exact words for every phrase.
- **A recommendation, when asked.** "How do we title-case headings?" was a question asking for a recommendation, not a request for the change. (The answer was sentence case.)
- **US spelling** in our own text from now on.
