### What a positioning note is (and why it matters)

A positioning note is a **one-page alignment artifact** for designers, developers, writers, and future collaborators.

It is *not* marketing copy and *not* a vision manifesto.
Its job is to ensure that everyone building or touching the product shares the same answers to:

* What is this?
* Who is it for?
* What problem does it exist to solve?
* What it is explicitly *not* trying to be.

Think of it as a **north star for decisions**, not something users ever see verbatim.

---

### How it differs from related things

* Not a tagline → too short and vague
* Not a blurb → those are outward-facing and promotional
* Not a strategy doc → too long and operational
* Not a vision statement → too abstract

A positioning note sits *between* vision and execution.

### Positioning note (draft)

### Reports that Matter

#### One-line description

Reports that Matter makes important public-interest reports discoverable, readable, and linkable on the web.

#### The problem we exist to solve

Governments publish high-quality reports on matters of public importance, but they are typically:

* hard to find,
* locked in large or broken PDFs,
* poorly indexed by search engines,
* impossible to link to at the level of specific findings.

As a result, these reports are underused in journalism, research, and public discourse.

#### What we do

We collect and present serious public reports in a web-native way so that they can be:

* searched as text,
* browsed online,
* linked to at paragraph or section level,
* reliably referenced over time.

We focus on access and structure, not interpretation.

#### Who this is for

Primary:

* Journalists
* Researchers
* Civic and policy actors
* Educated readers seeking primary sources

Secondary:

* Anyone arriving via search or shared excerpts who wants to see the original evidence.

#### What makes this different

* We do not summarise *instead of* the source; we point *into* it.
* We prioritise deep linking and permanence over commentary.
* We treat reports as long-lived public infrastructure, not news content.

#### What this is not

* Not a media outlet
* Not an advocacy platform
* Not an opinion or analysis site
* Not a general document dump

If a design or feature decision pushes the project toward those directions, it is likely wrong.

#### Tone and posture

* Serious, neutral, evidence-first
* Curious rather than persuasive
* Minimal branding; clarity over flourish

#### Success looks like

* People link directly to report passages instead of PDFs.
* Reports that were previously hard to access appear in search results.
* Journalists and researchers use the site as a reference point.

### How to use this

Include this note as:

* the **first page** of the creative/development brief, or
* a standalone README shared with anyone working on the project.

For the team:

> If you are unsure how to design or implement something, re-read this note and decide which option best supports *discoverability, readability, and linkability of reports*.
