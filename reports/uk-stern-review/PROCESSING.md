# Processing notes — Stern Review: The Economics of Climate Change

How the text on Reports that Matter was made, and where it still falls short of the published report. Nothing has been rewritten. Where we know the text differs from the published report, this page says so.

*Last reviewed 12 November 2026.*

## The edition

- **Source:** *Stern Review: The Economics of Climate Change*, the independent review led by Sir Nicholas Stern for the Chancellor of the Exchequer and the Prime Minister, published by HM Treasury on 30 October 2006 as a set of PDFs, one for each part of the report. HM Treasury's website of the time is held by the UK Government Web Archive; our copies are the Wayback Machine's captures of the same files. Each file is kept, with its SHA-256, in the [report's repository](https://github.com/reportsthatmatter/uk-stern-review).
- **Which printing:** HM Treasury replaced several of its PDFs in January 2007 with corrected files (for example, Chapter 10's "feasibl,e" became "feasible", and a note under Figure 10.1, "GWP should read GDP", was added). Where a corrected file exists we use it: the Preface and Acknowledgements, the Introduction, Chapters 2, 6, 9, 10 and 13 and the Part III introduction. The Executive Summary, the Summary of Conclusions and the Part I and II introductions were never replaced, so those are the files of October 2006.
- **Licence:** Crown copyright, reused under the Open Government Licence v3.0. The PDFs print no copyright line of their own; the Review was carried out and published by HM Treasury, and The National Archives states that Crown copyright material in the UK Government Web Archive may be reused under the Open Government Licence. The data in the figures and tables belong to the sources each one names.
- **Covers so far:** the Executive Summary; the Preface and Acknowledgements; the Introduction; the Summary of Conclusions; the introductions to Parts I, II and III; and five chapters: 2 (Economics, ethics and climate change), 6 (Economic modelling of climate-change impacts), 9 (Identifying the costs of mitigation), 10 (Macroeconomic models of costs) and 13 (Towards a goal for climate-change policy). They are in the order of the Review's own table of contents. The other chapters, Parts IV to VI, the technical annexes and the Postscript are not here yet. The book published by Cambridge University Press in 2007 is a separate edition and is not used.
- **Size:** 149 PDF pages, about 77,000 words, with 156 footnotes.

## How the text was read

- **Born-digital, so the words are the PDF's own.** The PDFs were made from Microsoft Word; their text layer is clean and no OCR is involved. About 94% of the PDFs' words are in the text here; the rest are the running heads and feet, and the words inside figures and tables (below).
- **Page numbers** are the printed ones. The Executive Summary is folioed i to xxvii; the Preface, Introduction and Summary of Conclusions start again at i and run to ix, so their pages appear as a second i to ix (in links, "i#2" and so on); the Parts run in arabic numbers, 1 to 575 across the whole report, so a chapter here starts at its printed page (Chapter 6 at 143).
- **Structure.** Each Part's title, each chapter's title and each numbered section ("6.4 Calculating the global cost of climate change") is a heading, read from the typeface the report sets them in. The key findings, set in bold italic at the head of the passages that argue them, are served in bold italic, as printed. Each chapter's "Key Messages" box is served as ordinary paragraphs under its title.
- **Footnotes.** Each chapter numbers its notes from 1, as printed; all 156 notes are read. The markers in the chapters are small raised numbers, linked to their notes by their position on the page. The "2" of "CO2", "CO2e" and "GtCO2e" is raised in the same way and is not a note marker; it is set on the line here. Where two markers are printed together ("wealth.89" for notes 8 and 9, Chapter 6) they were set by hand from the page.
- **Figures, tables and equations.** Figures and tables keep their title, their caption and their "Source:" line. The charts' labels and axes and the tables' rows are not served (below).
- **Corrections.** Five corrections, each checked against the page image: three compounds broken over a line end in the Executive Summary ("peer-reviewed", "co-benefits", "flood-resistant"); "co-operation" kept with its hyphen in the Executive Summary (p.xxiii), as the summary spells it throughout; and the last words of a note carried over a page (Chapter 9, note 17, pp.217-218) set back at the end of that note. Seventeen words the review queue flagged as possible misreadings ("CO2e", "4CMR", "Barnett" and others) were checked and are as printed.
- **Checked against the page.** Sixteen pages were read line by line against the page images: eight in the Executive Summary and eight in the new parts (the Introduction, the Summary of Conclusions, and pages from each chapter with figures, tables, lists and notes). Fourteen match exactly; the two that do not are listed below.

## Known limitations

- **The tables are not shown.** Each table's title and source are served, but not its rows. Among them is Table 6.1 (p.163), which sets out the losses in consumption, from 2.1% to 14.4%, behind the "5% to 20%" estimate; Tables 9.1 to 9.3 (pp.219, 233) on the costs of cutting emissions; Table 10.1 (p.243); and Tables 13.1 to 13.4 (pp.289, 295-297). Two notes are cited only from inside a table, and so appear in the notes with no marker in the text: Chapter 9's note 25 and Chapter 10's note 4. Read the tables in the PDFs.
- **The figures are not shown.** Each figure's title, caption and source are served; the charts are not. Figure 2 of the Executive Summary (p.v) and Figure 13.4 (p.294), which set the impacts expected at each level of warming against the stabilisation levels, are among them.
- **Equations.** The equations in Chapter 6's Box 6.3 (pp.161-162) are set in a mathematical type that does not survive as text: some symbols are missing, and the equations read as fragments ("W = ∑ U (t )e −δt (5)").
- **Lists over a page break.** Where a bulleted item runs over a page, its continuation can stand as a paragraph of its own: "(output-weighted), and they reach 0.5 - 2% of global GDP..." (Chapter 6, p.148) and the item on non-energy emissions in Chapter 9 (p.218). The list of models under Figure 6.2's caption (p.147) runs into the caption's paragraph.
- **Figure 1's title in the Executive Summary sits at the foot of page iii.** It is printed at the head of page iv, but the text here runs it on to the end of the paragraph before ("...exceeding 2°C. Figure 1 Greenhouse-gas emissions in 2000, by source"): the "C." of "2°C." is read as an initial, which normally carries a sentence over the page.
- **Subscripts are not shown.** "CO2e" and "GtCO2e" are printed with a subscript 2; the text here sets them on the line.
- **The Part titles carry a colon** ("Part I: Climate Change – our approach"), as the running heads print them; the Part introduction pages set the title over two lines without one.
- **The rest of the Review is not here yet:** Chapters 1, 3-5, 7, 8, 11, 12 and 14-27, the technical annexes and the Postscript.
