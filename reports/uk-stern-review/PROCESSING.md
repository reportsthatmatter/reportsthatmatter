# Processing notes — Stern Review: The Economics of Climate Change

How the text on Reports that Matter was made, and where it still falls short of the published report. Nothing has been rewritten. Where we know the text differs from the published report, this page says so.

*Last reviewed 13 November 2026.*

## The edition

- **Source:** *Stern Review: The Economics of Climate Change*, the independent review led by Sir Nicholas Stern for the Chancellor of the Exchequer and the Prime Minister, published by HM Treasury on 30 October 2006 as a set of PDFs, one for each part of the report. HM Treasury's website of the time is held by the UK Government Web Archive; our copies are the Wayback Machine's captures of the same files. Each file is kept, with its SHA-256, in the [report's repository](https://github.com/reportsthatmatter/uk-stern-review).
- **Which printing:** HM Treasury replaced several of its PDFs in January 2007 with corrected files (for example, Chapter 10's "feasibl,e" became "feasible", and a note under Figure 10.1, "GWP should read GDP", was added). Where a corrected file exists we use it: the Preface and Acknowledgements, the Introduction, the Part III introduction and Chapters 2, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 17, 18, 19, 21, 23, 24 and 27. For Chapters 1, 3, 5, 7, 8, 9, 15, 16, 20, 22 and 25 the January 2007 file is the only one archived. The Executive Summary, the Summary of Conclusions, the Part I, II, IV, V and VI introductions and Chapters 4, 14 and 26 were never replaced, so those are the files of October 2006. HM Treasury's reissue of Chapter 11 rewords a few sentences as well as correcting them; the text here is the reissue's.
- **Licence:** Crown copyright, reused under the Open Government Licence v3.0. The PDFs print no copyright line of their own; the Review was carried out and published by HM Treasury, and The National Archives states that Crown copyright material in the UK Government Web Archive may be reused under the Open Government Licence. The data in the figures and tables belong to the sources each one names.
- **Covers so far:** the Executive Summary; the Preface and Acknowledgements; the Introduction; the Summary of Conclusions; the introductions to Parts I, II and III; and seventeen chapters: 1 (The science of climate change), 2 (Economics, ethics and climate change), 3 (How climate change will affect people around the world), 4 (Implications of climate change for development), 5 (Costs of climate change in developed countries), 6 (Economic modelling of climate-change impacts), 7 (Projecting the growth of greenhouse-gas emissions, with Annex 7A), 8 (The challenge of stabilisation), 9 (Identifying the costs of mitigation), 10 (Macroeconomic models of costs), 11 (Structural change and competitiveness, with Annex 11A), 12 (Opportunities and wider benefits from climate policies) and 13 (Towards a goal for climate-change policy); and Part IV, Policy responses for mitigation: its introduction and Chapters 14 (Harnessing markets for mitigation: the role of taxation and trading), 15 (Carbon pricing and emissions markets in practice), 16 (Accelerating technological innovation) and 17 (Beyond carbon markets and technology); Part V, Policy responses for adaptation: its introduction and Chapters 18 (Understanding the economics of adaptation), 19 (Adaptation in the developed world) and 20 (Adaptation in the developing world); and Part VI, International collective action: its introduction and Chapters 21 (Framework for understanding international collective action for climate change), 22 (Creating a global price for carbon), 23 (Supporting the transition to a low-carbon global economy), 24 (Promoting effective international technology co-operation), 25 (Reversing emissions from land use change), 26 (International support for adaptation) and 27 (Conclusions: building and sustaining international co-operation on climate change). They are in the order of the Review's own table of contents. The technical annexes and the Postscript are not here yet. The book published by Cambridge University Press in 2007 is a separate edition and is not used.
- **Size:** 597 PDF pages, about 303,000 words, with 1,237 footnotes.

## How the text was read

- **Born-digital, so the words are the PDF's own.** The PDFs were made from Microsoft Word; their text layer is clean and no OCR is involved. About 93% of the PDFs' words are in the text here; the rest are the running heads and feet, and the words inside figures and tables (below).
- **Page numbers** are the printed ones. The Executive Summary is folioed i to xxvii; the Preface, Introduction and Summary of Conclusions start again at i and run to ix, so their pages appear as a second i to ix (in links, "i#2" and so on); the Parts run in arabic numbers through the whole report (1 to 575 in its table of contents), so a chapter here starts at its printed page (Chapter 6 at 143). One exception: HM Treasury's corrected file of Chapter 5 prints its pages 1 to 21, where the table of contents sets the chapter at pp.122-142. The text keeps the printed numbers, so a Chapter 5 page here is numbered as the file prints it (in links, "1#2" and so on, after Part I's pages 1 to 21); add 121 for the Review's own page. HM Treasury's corrected file of Chapter 11 does the same, printing pages 1 to 16 for the table of contents' pp.253-268 (in links, "1#3" and so on); add 252. The Part IV introduction prints p.308, the number of Chapter 13's last page, as the table of contents sets both; it appears here as a second 308 ("308#2"). HM Treasury's corrected file of Chapter 23 prints pages 1 to 25 for the table of contents' pp.491-515 (in links, "1#4" and so on); add 490.
- **Structure.** Each Part's title, each chapter's title and each numbered section ("6.4 Calculating the global cost of climate change") is a heading, read from the typeface the report sets them in. Chapter 26 prints a full stop at the end of one heading ("26.5 ... resettling refugees.", p.566); it is served without it. The key findings, set in bold italic at the head of the passages that argue them, are served in bold italic, as printed. Each chapter's "Key Messages" box is served as ordinary paragraphs under its title.
- **Footnotes.** Each chapter numbers its notes from 1, as printed; all 1,237 notes are read. The markers in the chapters are small raised numbers, linked to their notes by their position on the page. The "2" of "CO2", "CO2e" and "GtCO2e" is raised in the same way and is not a note marker; it is set on the line here. Where two markers are printed together ("wealth.89" for notes 8 and 9, Chapter 6), or a marker stands apart from its words ("greater than 5°C 19 .", Chapter 1), they were set by hand from the page.
- **Figures, tables and equations.** Figures and tables keep their title, their caption and their "Source:" line. The charts' labels and axes and the tables' rows are not served (below). In the chapters added in November 2026, a figure or table is found by its own title and the source line under it, and between them only the lines that read as prose are kept; where a figure cites its source in a note (Chapter 16), or sits in a box with no numbered title (Chapters 11, 14 and 15), its lines are found by the printed lines either side of it. In Parts V and VI the same holds for the tables and charts with no source line (Table 18.1, the diagrams and tables of Boxes 18.1 and 18.2, Table 21.1, Figures 21.1 and 21.2, the charts in Box 21.5 and on p.475, Box 21.6's survey, Table 24.1's second page and the chart in Box 24.8).
- **Corrections.** Ninety-six corrections, each checked against the page image: three compounds broken over a line end in the Executive Summary ("peer-reviewed", "co-benefits", "flood-resistant"); "co-operation" (p.xxiii) and "co-ordination" (p.xxv) kept with their hyphens in the Executive Summary, as the summary spells them; the last words of two notes carried over a page (Chapter 9, note 17, pp.217-218; Chapter 11, note 5, pp.255-256) set back at the end of their notes; "2⁰C", printed with a raised zero for the degree sign (Chapter 12, p.276), served as 2°C; "policy-makers" kept with its hyphen where it breaks over a line in Chapter 13, as the Review spells it; and sixty-one compounds, names, ranges and web addresses broken over a line end in the later volumes ("think-tanks", "500-550ppm", "2-3°C", "Muir-Wood", "South-East Asia", "non-CO2", "sub-Saharan", "self-perpetuating", "start-ups", "state-owned", "www.hm-treasury.gov.uk"), and twenty-four more in Parts V and VI ("like-minded", "non-OECD", "post-2012", "self-financing", "SD-PAMs", "willingness-to-pay", "www-wds.worldbank.org"); and "under-investment" kept with its hyphen where it breaks over a line in Chapter 12 (p.275), as the Review spells it, after Parts V and VI brought "underinvestment" into the text. Seventeen words the review queue flagged as possible misreadings ("CO2e", "4CMR", "Barnett" and others) were checked and are as printed.
- **Checked against the page.** Twenty-two pages were read line by line against the page images: eight in the Executive Summary and fourteen in the later parts (the Introduction, the Summary of Conclusions, and pages from the chapters with figures, tables, boxes, lists and notes). Twenty match exactly; the two that do not (pp.iii and iv) show the same defect, listed below.

## Known limitations

- **The tables are not shown.** Each table's title and source are served, but not its rows. Among them are Table 3.1 (p.57), the Review's summary of the impacts expected at each degree of warming; Table 1.1 (p.12), the temperatures expected at each stabilisation level; Table 6.1 (p.163), the losses in consumption under six scenarios (mean losses of 2.1% to 14.4%, and 0.3% to 32.6% from the 5th to the 95th percentile), from which Chapter 6 draws its 5% and 20% figures; Tables 8.1 and 8.2 (pp.198, 200); Tables 9.1 to 9.3 (pp.219, 233) on the costs of cutting emissions; Table 10.1 (p.243); Tables 13.1 to 13.4 (pp.289, 295-297); Table 18.1 (p.406), examples of adaptation; Table 21.1 (p.456), the climate and energy goals of the ten largest economies; and Table 24.1 (pp.528-529), the carbon price implied by deployment support. Table 3.2 (p.71), set in two columns of prose, and the small tables in Boxes 3.2 (p.61) and 8.4 (p.205) are not served either. Four notes are cited only from inside a table, and so appear in the notes with no marker in the text: Chapter 3's notes 9 and 10, Chapter 9's note 25 and Chapter 10's note 4. Chapter 21's note 15 is cited inside Table 21.1 and its text is printed on no page, so its number is missing from the chapter's run. So are the tables of Part IV and Annex table 11A.1 (pp.267-268), the Annex's key statistics for 123 UK production sectors (its title and note on trade intensity are served; the title of its second page runs on to the first's). Read the tables in the PDFs.
- **The figures are not shown.** Each figure's title, caption and source are served; the charts are not, nor the words set inside them. Figure 2 of the Executive Summary (p.v) and Figure 13.4 (p.294), which set the impacts expected at each level of warming against the stabilisation levels, are among them, and so is the text set in two columns inside Figure 8.5 (p.207), Socolow and Pacala's "wedges" and the four measures it lists. A few charts' labels still stand as a short line under their title ("Europe North America Australasia", Chapter 5).
- **Equations.** The equations in Chapter 6's Box 6.3 (pp.161-162) are set in a mathematical type that does not survive as text: some symbols are missing, and the equations read as fragments ("W = ∑ U (t )e −δt (5)").
- **Lists over a page break, and some boxed lists.** Where a bulleted item runs over a page, its continuation can stand as a paragraph of its own: "(output-weighted), and they reach 0.5 - 2% of global GDP..." (Chapter 6, p.148), the item on non-energy emissions in Chapter 9 (p.218) and a paragraph of Box 4.6 (pp.113-114). The list of models under Figure 6.2's caption (p.147) runs into the caption's paragraph. In the Key Messages of Chapters 3 and 5 (p.56; Chapter 5's first page), in Box 4.6 (p.113) and Box 5.2 (Chapter 5, pp.7-8) the bullets are served as "•" inside the paragraph, with the items run together. In Chapters 19 (p.421), 23 (its p.6) and 24 (p.525) some bulleted items stand as paragraphs opening on "•"; in Chapters 25 (pp.547-551) and 26 (p.564) the bold-italic lead of a bulleted item is served in bold italic with its bullet, and in Chapter 26 the bold italic can run on a few words past the lead.
- **Figure 1's title in the Executive Summary sits at the foot of page iii.** It is printed at the head of page iv, but the text here runs it on to the end of the paragraph before ("...exceeding 2°C. Figure 1 Greenhouse-gas emissions in 2000, by source"): the "C." of "2°C." is read as an initial, which normally carries a sentence over the page.
- **Subscripts and superscripts are not shown.** "CO2e" and "GtCO2e" are printed with a subscript 2, and "km2" and "m3" with a superscript; the text here sets them on the line.
- **The Part titles carry a colon** ("Part I: Climate Change – our approach"), as the running heads print them; the Part introduction pages set the title over two lines without one.
- **Annexes 7A and 11A are not headings.** Each is served as paragraphs under its chapter's References, with no entry in the contents.
- **The rest of the Review is not here yet:** the technical annexes and the Postscript.
